Process

Five steps, no surprises

The structure is agreed before any design work starts, so you review logic first and visuals second.

  1. Brief

    First, share the project purpose, audience, page count and deadline. Do not send patient identifiers or confidential records in your first email. I confirm the scope, file-handling approach and quote.

  2. Outline

    A slide-by-slide outline that maps your project to the QC Story / PDCA steps. You approve it.

  3. Draft

    Full design with native charts built from your data, on your template or a clean house style.

  4. QC

    A separate review pass checks numbers, labels, consistency and layout before you see it.

  5. Delivery

    Editable .pptx, PDF and the chart workbook. Two revision rounds are included.

FAQ

Common questions

How do I turn a cluttered PDSA spreadsheet into an A0 QI conference poster?

Start from the story, not the spreadsheet: aim statement, baseline, change ideas tested in PDSA cycles, run chart of the outcome measure, and lessons learned. I rebuild the key measures as native, editable charts (run or control chart, Pareto, before/after), cut the table to the few numbers that support the aim, and lay the poster out on the organizer's template (A0, 48 × 36 in or custom). Your team supplies and approves all clinical content and data.

What should a hospital QC circle (QC Story) presentation include?

A standard QC Story runs: theme selection, activity plan, current-state survey, target setting, root-cause analysis (fishbone, Pareto), countermeasures, implementation, effect confirmation, standardization and review. I check that the steps, analysis tools and data agree with each other before visual design starts, and deliver an editable .pptx with native charts plus a PDF.

Who designs QI posters and QC circle decks for nurses and hospital quality teams?

Shi Zhang Studio is an independent healthcare and technical presentation consultancy (UTC+8) that designs QC Story / PDCA / A3 decks, QI and patient-safety conference posters and medical-education slides from client-approved materials, for hospital teams and as white-label production for consultancies and presentation agencies. Scope and a flat estimate are agreed by email before work starts.

What is the difference between a QI poster, an ePoster and a 5-minute oral deck?

A printed poster (usually A0 or 48 × 36 in) must be readable from 1–2 metres with a single main chart; an ePoster is the same story re-flowed for a 16:9 screen, often with one slide per section; a 5-minute oral deck needs 5–7 slides with one message each. The poster pack delivers all three from one approved content source.
